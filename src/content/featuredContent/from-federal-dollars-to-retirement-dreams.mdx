What does retirement look like to you?

Maybe it’s traveling across the country, starting a business, spending more time with family, or simply having peace of mind about the years ahead. Retirement looks different for everyone, but planning for it often involves more than personal savings.

Did you know federal programs also play an important role in supporting millions of Americans?

Whether retirement is right around the corner or still years away, understanding the programs that help provide financial security can offer valuable insight into the bigger picture.

While [USAspending.gov](https://www.usaspending.gov/) isn’t a personal financial planner, it provides the public with transparent data on federal spending, including programs like Social Security. By exploring spending trends and how federal dollars support these programs, you can gain a better understanding of the government's role in supporting Americans during retirement and beyond.

## The Social Security Program

The Social Security program, established in 1935, created a national system of retirement benefits at a time when many older Americans relied primarily on personal savings, family members, or community support.

Today, Social Security provides benefits to retirees, people with disabilities, and eligible family members. The Social Security Administration (SSA) serves more than [70 million beneficiaries](https://www.ssa.gov/oact/TR/2026/) each month, making it one of the largest and most relied-upon federal programs.

The program’s size is reflected in its federal spending. In fiscal year (FY) 2025, the Social Security Administration spent [$1.7 trillion](https://www.usaspending.gov/agency/social-security-administration?fy=2025), 13.1% of the FY 2025 U.S. federal budget. That made the SSA one of the top federal agencies in terms of total spending.

## Following the Federal Dollars
USAspending makes it easy to explore the data behind federal spending, understand how federal programs work, and learn how government resources are managed. Begin with [SSA](https://www.ssa.gov/) to track how federal dollars support retirement benefits and then use USAspending tools to explore trends and structure. You can learn more by visiting the resources below.

### Agency Profiles
[Agency profiles](https://www.usaspending.gov/agency) show how federal departments and agencies receive and spend funding over time. This view is helpful if you want to see how agencies like the [Social Security Administration](https://www.usaspending.gov/agency/social-security-administration?fy=2026) operate, what their priorities are, and how spending has changed across fiscal years.

On the agency profile pages, you can view a fiscal year breakdown that shows:
-	Available funding for the agency
-	Planned agency spending
-	Planned spending on awards

### Federal Accounts
<GlossaryLink term="federal-account" label="Federal Accounts" /> provide a detailed look at how funds are structured and allocated. See how SSA organizes funds into accounts that support benefits and operations—similar to how you might separate your savings for different goals such as “vacation” or “home repairs”.

### Spending Explorer
Want to see the bigger picture? Explore SSA obligations on the [Spending Explorer](https://www.usaspending.gov/explorer) by <GlossaryLink term="object-class" label="object class" /> or budget function to understand the trends in spending that support national retirement benefits.

If you [explore by agency](https://www.usaspending.gov/explorer/agency), you will notice the Social Security Administration makes up a large portion of the federal government’s spending obligations.

Take a closer look at the federal dollars behind Social Security on [USAspending.gov](https://www.usaspending.gov/). You may be surprised by what the data reveals.
