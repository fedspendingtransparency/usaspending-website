The phrase “government financial transparency” has become a modern buzzword, dominating headlines and conversations across all forms of media and at every level of government.

Whether you’re wondering where your community is getting the money for a new swing set at the local park or curious about how much of the federal budget is going toward Social Security, knowing where and how your government spends its money is the key to making informed decisions as an active participant in democracy. 

But did you know that government financial transparency isn’t a “new” invention? In fact, it’s written into the U.S. Constitution!

## Constitutional Mandate for Transparency

The creators of the U.S. Constitution understood that democracy requires accountability; [Article I, Section 9](https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/constitution-transcript) mandates a "regular Statement and Account" of how public funds are collected and spent. Mandates like this one established transparency as a fundamental principle of American governance.

In 1793, the U.S. Treasury published the first [Account of Receipts & Expenditures](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/GOVPUB-T-075642376e880d28991a9dad9a20ef93). This  document represented a revolutionary idea: citizens have the right to know how their government spends their money.

## Today's Digital Transparency

Fast forward to today, and that single annual report has evolved into vast digital ecosystems of federal financial information. 

In 2006, Congress passed the [Federal Funding Accountability and Transparency Act (FFATA)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-109publ282/pdf/PLAW-109publ282.pdf) with a simple but transformative goal: give Americans an easy way to see how their tax dollars are spent. And one year later, [USAspending.gov](https://www.usaspending.gov/) launched in 2007, making detailed federal award level data available to the public for the first time.

To learn more about the early days of USAspending and the laws that got us here, read our Featured Content article “[The Story of Spending Transparency](https://www.usaspending.gov/featured-content/data-you-can-trust/the-story-of-spending-transparency).”

USAspending and [FiscalData.Treasury.gov](https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/) provide unprecedented access to government spending data. Users just like you can now track everything from government contracts and grants to national debt figures and revenue streams, all from your smartphone!

“Government transparency” once meant an annual printed statement that few had the ability to access or read. It now means current spending information that’s available at your fingertips through data sets that anyone can download, analyze, and share.

## You Can Follow the Money

The tools have changed dramatically since 1793, but the principle remains constant: in a democracy, the people have the right to follow the money. 

Exercise your right to know. Visit [USAspending.gov](https://www.usaspending.gov/) and [FiscalData.Treasury.gov](https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/) today and explore how your government manages its money (and yours!).
