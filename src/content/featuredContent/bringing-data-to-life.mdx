## Attention: Infrastructure!
Planes, trains, and automobiles can get you where you need to go. But what if there’s no roads, bridges, or airports? America’s infrastructure is a key part of transportation that’s often overlooked by the average person.

“Infrastructure” is a word used to describe systems and facilities like highways, drinking water systems, and energy. But how much do governments invest in things like this?

A simple [USAspending.gov search](https://www.usaspending.gov/search) for the [Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act](https://www.congress.gov/bill/117th-congress/house-bill/3684) shows that in Fiscal Year 2025, the U.S. awarded [$187.6B](https://www.usaspending.gov/search?hash=33228179bd72f92d81811df275b956a4) in new funding for infrastructure projects.

Very few people look at a smooth interstate on-ramp and think, “I funded that infrastructure.”

Ever hit a pothole going 60 miles an hour on the highway? That’s hard to miss.

When a government doesn’t invest in infrastructure, it can result in bumpy roads, crumbling bridges, and even overcrowded airports. Poor infrastructure often goes unnoticed, but when it fails, the impacts can range from inconvenient to dangerous. 

[George Mason College of Engineering and Computing](https://cec.gmu.edu/) hosted a hackathon (an innovation event where teams work to create a solution to a problem) using data from The U.S. Department of the Treasury Bureau of the Fiscal Service’s [USAspending.gov](https://www.usaspending.gov/) website. The hackathon, titled "Bringing USAspending Data to Life: Connecting Federal Investments to Everyday Experiences," challenged students to find creative and accessible ways to connect government spending data directly to the environments, landmarks, and infrastructure projects people see every day.
